What constrains what we can claim

Timestamps carry no behavioural signal D-009

All 50 sessions start exactly on the hour, and every one of them runs at exactly 42 s per turn (duration_min == 0.7 × n_turns). Source: timing_summary.csv Session duration, time-to-reply and time-of-day therefore tell us nothing about engagement. Timestamps are UTC with no user timezone, so we can't infer "late-night use" either.

Only 2 of the 4 documented end states occur brief vs data

completed 40, user_closed 10. There are no timeout or user_asked_for_human sessions, so we can't measure escalation to humans from the label. If escalation needs exist, we'll have to find them in the text.

session_ended_by doesn't match how conversations actually end D-010

13 sessions end on a user turn (7 completed, 6 user_closed), and 4 user_closed sessions end on an assistant turn. Source: ending_crosstab.csv

The label on its own is a weak proxy for success. Treat it as one signal, not the outcome.

Almost everything is in English, with one telling exception

The conversations are in English, including those from Japan, Brazil, France, Germany, Italy and India. The exception is s018 (Brazil): the user switches into Portuguese as they get frustrated with the bot.

t9: "i dont have 'a plan'. porque você não entende? no plan. no network. i explain already"
t23: "insurance directory again... esquece. você não vai entender"

In s008 (France) the user says "médecin traitant", and the bot mirrors the term back. Our detector only looks for non-ASCII letters, so it would miss non-English text written without accents; all 6 hits in non_ascii_turns.csv were read by hand. s018 is a strong candidate for the "interesting five": a cultural-fit failure (the bot keeps assuming a US-style insurance plan) that shows up as a language switch.

Correction: the first version of this doc said "zero non-ASCII letters". That came from filtering at a 5% share, which hid these turns. The code-reviewer agent caught it.

Early signals worth following up

The bot out-talks the user ~3.3 to 1

This is the median assistant/user word ratio; the range is 1.1–8.3. The widest-ratio sessions deserve a qualitative look: is the bot over-explaining to someone who writes briefly?

Clinical accuracy moment: s003 (UC, US)

The bot first gave a mesalamine dose of 4.8 g, then corrected itself:

"I'm sorry I muddied the water by throwing out 4.8g first, since that's really a flare-induction number and not a universal target."

This is a candidate for the safety dimension and for the "five interesting conversations".
